# Guideline bodies: mechanism, calendar and financial linkage

| Body | Output | Financial mechanism | Calendar |
|---|---|---|---|
| **USPSTF** | Grades A, B, C, D, I for preventive services | A/B obliges most non-grandfathered private plans to cover with no cost sharing (ACA §2713), generally from the plan year beginning after the recommendation | Draft research plan → draft recommendation with public comment → final. Each stage published; watch the draft |
| **ACIP** | Vaccine recommendations, voted | CDC director adoption drives private coverage and the Vaccines for Children programme | Regular public meetings with agendas and materials posted in advance; votes are webcast |
| **NCCN** | Category 1, 2A, 2B, 3 listings | CMS and most commercial payers reference the compendia for coverage of off-label use in oncology | Panel updates published continuously; version numbers change through the year |
| **ADA** | Standards of Care | Practice-shaping, no mandate | Published each January |
| **ACC/AHA** | Clinical practice guidelines | Practice-shaping, no mandate | Irregular; often released at the annual scientific sessions |
| **GOLD / KDIGO / GINA** | Disease-specific strategy documents | Practice-shaping, global reach | Annual or periodic |

## What makes a guideline catalyst tradeable

1. **A cost-sharing change at a fixed date.** This is the only mechanism that produces
   something close to a step change, and it is why USPSTF A/B grades matter more than
   any society guideline.
2. **A compendia listing that unlocks reimbursement** for a use that was previously
   paid out of pocket or denied.
3. **A vaccine schedule addition**, which brings both private coverage and VFC volume.

Everything else is a ramp, and a slow one. Published work on guideline implementation
consistently finds long lags between publication and practice change.

## The governance overlay (2025 onward)

The historical reliability of the USPSTF and ACIP linkage rested on those panels
operating as independent expert bodies with stable membership. During 2025 the
membership and process of both were disrupted. Practical consequences for modelling:

- A recommendation may be revisited or reversed on a shorter horizon than historically.
- The coverage consequence may be litigated rather than implemented.
- The *direction* of a recommendation is now less predictable from the evidence base
  alone, because panel composition carries more weight.

Do not remove the linkage from the model — it remains the mechanism. Handicap it, state
the handicap explicitly, and name the next panel meeting as the follow-up observable.

## Framework anchors

Greenhalgh, *How to Read a Paper*, and Guyatt, *Users' Guides to the Medical
Literature*, for reading the evidence statement behind a recommendation — in particular
for judging whether the evidence base is strong enough that the recommendation is likely
to survive a change of panel.
