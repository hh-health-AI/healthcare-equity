# Device & Asset Development Checkpoints

Stage model for placing an asset on the risk curve. Distilled for diligence use from the development literature in the project KB (*Medical Device Design — Innovation From Concept to Market*; *Commercializing Successful Biomedical Technologies*; *Humanizing Healthcare — Human Factors*) — `project_search` those for depth when attached to the Healthcare Plugin project.

## The maturation ladder (what "on track" looks like)

1. **Concept & feasibility** — defined user need and clinical use case; bench feasibility; provisional IP filed. Risk: solving a problem clinicians don't rank.
2. **Design & development (design controls)** — documented user needs → design inputs → outputs → verification (did we build it right) → validation (did we build the right thing), under a quality system (design history file, risk management per ISO 14971). Skipped or retrofitted design controls surface later as FDA findings and recalls.
3. **Preclinical/bench + biocompatibility** — performance testing against recognized standards; animal data where required.
4. **Human factors validation** — the HFE gate: simulated-use studies with representative users on critical tasks; FDA expects use-related risk analysis and validation for devices where use error is dangerous. A device failing usability validation late loses quarters, not weeks — and usability drives adoption, not just approval (demand-side half lives in the provider-adoption plugin).
5. **Clinical evidence** — pathway-dependent: bench-only (many 510(k)s) → small confirmatory studies (De Novo) → pivotal IDE trials (PMA). Endpoint choice and comparator here set the payor-evidence bar later.
6. **Regulatory submission & review** — see fda-pathways-fto.md.
7. **Manufacturing scale-up & quality** — design transfer, process validation, supplier qualification; FDA inspection readiness. For drugs/biologics this is the CMC track: validation batches, yield, capacity, release testing (translate per the CMC evidence row — moves launch probability/timing/margin/capital, not demand).
8. **Market entry stack** — regulatory clearance ≠ revenue: still need coding (procedure-exposure plugin), coverage (cms-reimbursement), payment level, guideline position, and site-of-care economics. The classic commercialization failure is a cleared device with no reimbursement pathway.
9. **Evidence expansion & lifecycle** — post-market studies, registry data, label/claim expansion, next-gen iterations; recalls and MDRs as ongoing tail risk.

## Diligence checkpoints per stage

For any device story, establish: which stage the lead product actually occupies (with document evidence, not deck claims) · what the next gate is and its date · what evidence the gate requires · what the company has disclosed about prior gate attempts (repeat submissions, deficiency letters) · whether reimbursement work is running in parallel or deferred (deferred = timeline risk).
