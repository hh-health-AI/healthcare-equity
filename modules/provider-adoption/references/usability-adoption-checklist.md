# Usability as an Adoption Driver — demand-side checklist

Distilled from human-factors and commercialization literature in the project KB (*Humanizing Healthcare — Human Factors for Medical Device*; *Commercializing successful biomedical technologies*) — `project_search` for depth. The regulatory half (HFE validation as an approval gate) lives in the clinical-catalysts plugin; this is the demand-side half: usability decides whether cleared products get used.

## Why it moves the model

Use difficulty taxes every stage of the funnel: initiation (setup/training friction), throughput (case time, room turnover), persistence (patient-facing complexity drives discontinuation), and site economics (staffing intensity per case). Per the evidence-translation rules, "easier administration" moves initiation, site of care, throughput, and persistence — not efficacy or TAM.

## Checklist — score the product a channel check can verify

**Clinician-side:** learning curve (cases to proficiency; proctoring required?) · workflow fit (does it add steps or roles to the existing pathway?) · case time vs incumbent · failure/error modes under real conditions (interruptions, staffing gaps — not IFU conditions) · cognitive load (alarms, screens, modes) · cross-coverage (can partners/mid-levels use it, or does one trained champion gate all volume?).

**Site-side:** staffing per case vs incumbent · room/chair time · setup, reprocessing, disposables logistics · IT/EHR integration burden · service and downtime experience.

**Patient-side (self-administered products):** steps to correct use · dexterity/vision/literacy assumptions vs the actual population · at-home failure consequences · support burden (call centers, nurse educators).

## Signals of usability-driven adoption risk

Volume concentrated in champion users with weak spread across partners; training backlog cited as growth constraint; utilization per site flat while site count grows (bought but shelved — cross-check procedure-exposure); early-adopter enthusiasm with community-site indifference; version churn addressing use errors; MAUDE/complaint patterns clustering on use error rather than device failure.

## Question bank for expert calls (MNPI-safe)

"Walk me through a typical case with it — where does time go vs your old approach?" · "How many cases before you felt proficient, and who else in your group uses it?" · "What breaks on a bad day?" · "If it disappeared tomorrow, what changes for you?" · "What would make you use it twice as often?"
